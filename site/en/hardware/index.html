Before you install

Hardware, and what I measured

Raye has been tried on one virtual machine and a single laptop. That's not much, and I'd rather say so before the first figure.

What you need

  • A 64-bit Intel or AMD processor, and a PC that starts in UEFI mode (most PCs since 2012) or the older BIOS mode, MBR disks too: see Older PCs.
  • 8 GB of memory recommended. The installer accepts less, but I haven't tried Raye on a 4 GB PC.
  • 25 GB of free disk space: that's what the installer itself demands.
  • A USB stick of 8 GB or more to install.
  • Secure Boot and a TPM chip are supported, not required.

Read each figure next to the machine it came from. The test machine has no graphics card: the whole desktop is drawn by the processor there, which swells its memory. About 120 MB of the 850 MB are a kernel reserve that grows with the machine's memory. The figure is taken three minutes after signing in, with free -m.

The test machine's firmware reads the disk at about 7 MB/s: its startup times are for comparing two versions, not for promising you a speed. And after the Raye picture, a second black screen of 3 to 5 s remains before the sign-in screen at every startup.

My goal was 1 GB of memory at idle: the test machine is now under it. The GB and MB on this site are counted the way Windows counts them, by 1,024.

The machines tried

The test machine: a virtual machine with 8 cores and 16 GB of memory, no graphics card, two virtual screens (1280×800 and 1920×1080), Secure Boot on and a TPM 2.0 chip. That's where I install a version from scratch and update it from the previous one, before it goes out. I haven't always done it: the two rounds of fixes of 0.2 Beta 2 only got the update test.

A Dell Latitude 5420, 16 GB, with its 1366×768 screen and an external 1920×1080 screen over HDMI, Intel Wi-Fi and Ethernet. Raye has run on it since November 1, 2026, and gets its updates there.

What is still missing

  • On the Latitude, sleep sometimes fails, and Wi-Fi and Ethernet are slow to come back after sleep.
  • Updating its BIOS through Raye Update failed once, for want of a piece added since; I haven't seen it succeed yet.

What I haven't tried

No desktop PC, no AMD or NVIDIA graphics card, no laptop of another brand, no 4 GB PC. I haven't measured Raye against Windows on the same PC, or startup on a real NVMe disk, or battery life, or a game. If your PC isn't on this page, which is true of almost all of them, tell me how it went: bonjour@rayelinux.com.
