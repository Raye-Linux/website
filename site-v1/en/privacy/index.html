Privacy policy

Last updated: November 20, 2026 · version 4.0

Version prepared for the V1 site. It will apply when that site goes online; until then, version 3.0 published on rayelinux.com is the one in force.

In short

  • I keep as little as I can, I sell nothing, and there are no ads, no tracking and no cookies.
  • The site, the help bubble and the mailing list run on my own servers in Québec. Connections go through Cloudflare (United States).
  • The mailing list keeps your address, your language and the proof of your consent, until you unsubscribe.
  • Help bubble questions are not kept. Published bug reports go to our Discord server; how to reach you is never posted there.
  • An application to join the team keeps your email, your Discord name and your text, while it is reviewed; your email is never sent to Discord.
  • Raye Linux sends no usage reports and no crash reports from your PC.
  • You can see, correct or delete everything: write to bonjour@rayelinux.com, answer within 30 days.

What this policy covers

This policy says what personal information is collected when you use the rayelinux.com website, its help bubble, its bug report form, its mailing list, our Discord server and the Raye Linux software, why, where it goes, how long it stays and how to use your rights. It is written from the code and the servers as they really work, and it also says what isn't right yet.

It follows Québec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector ("Law 25") and, for people in the European Union, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). If a law of your country gives you more rights, those rights apply too.

Words used here

  • Personal information: any information about a person that identifies them, directly or not (an email address, an IP address, a Discord name).
  • I, me: the person who makes Raye Linux and this site, introduced below. We, the team: that person and the people who moderate the Discord server with them.
  • The site: rayelinux.com and its pages, in French under /fr/ and in English under /en/.
  • The help bubble: the "Help" button at the bottom of the pages, which answers questions and holds the "Report a bug" form.
  • Raye or the software: the Raye Linux system, as you download and install it.
  • Service provider: a company that handles information for me or on the path of your data (Cloudflare, Contabo, Discord).

Who is responsible and how to reach me

Raye Linux and this site are made by one person in Québec, under the pseudonym "techoraye". That person decides what is collected and why, and is also the person in charge of protecting personal information under Law 25.

For any question, request or complaint about your information: bonjour@rayelinux.com. Write in French or English. No representative is appointed in the European Union.

The mailing list

What is kept

If you sign up, I keep your email address, the language you chose, whether you want occasional news, the date you signed up and confirmed, and the version of the consent text you agreed to. To send the emails, the server also keeps, while sending, a queue of messages to go out and, only as a hash, the tokens of the confirmation and unsubscribe links: those links are never stored in a readable form.

Your IP address is not stored with the signup: it is only used, in memory and for an hour at most, to block abuse (5 signups per hour per address at most).

Why, and on what basis

To write to you at launch and, only if you asked for it, a bit of occasional project news. The basis is your express consent, given by ticking the box and confirmed by the link I email you (double opt-in). You can withdraw it at any time.

How long

  • An unconfirmed signup is deleted 48 hours after the confirmation email is sent (7 days at most if that email could not go out).
  • A confirmed address is kept until you unsubscribe or ask me to delete it.
  • An unsubscribed address is deleted within 30 days. The delay is only there so you aren't signed up again by mistake.
  • The list exists in one place only: there is no backup copy of it.

How the emails go out

Emails leave from my own mail server in Québec and go out to the Internet through a server I rent from Contabo, in Germany, linked to my home by an encrypted tunnel: the message passes through it and is not kept there. Your email provider then receives the message, under its own rules.

Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link. No email holds a tracking pixel or a tracked link.

The help bubble

Questions asked in the help bubble go to a language model running on my own server in Québec. It answers from the Raye handbook, the site's pages and the release notes. The questions are not sent to any other company, and are not used to train a model.

Neither your questions nor the answers are kept: not on disk, not in the logs. The server only counts how many questions it got. The conversation lives in the open page and is gone when you close it or reload the page. Your IP address is used, in memory and for 10 minutes at most, to limit the number of questions (20 in any 10 minutes).

The basis: you ask for an answer and I give it (your request; under the GDPR, performing the service you asked for). The answers are produced automatically and can be wrong: when in doubt, ask a person on Discord. Don't put personal information in your questions: they pass through Cloudflare like the rest of the site.

Reporting a bug

What the form sends

The "Report a bug" form in the help bubble sends my server what you write in it (what happened, how to make it happen again, and how to reach you if you give that), the screenshot if you add one, the address of the page, the page's language and the name of your browser. The screenshot is shrunk in your browser before it is sent. Every field except "what happened" is optional.

The check before posting

Before anything is posted, the report is checked by filters and by the help bubble's language model, on my own server; nothing is sent to a third party for this check. Depending on the result, the report is:

  • posted in the #bugs forum of our Discord server, if it is clearly a bug;
  • held for a person to read, in a private staff channel: text too short, contact details in the text, a link, a mention, swearing, a screenshot, a doubt from the model, or the model not answering;
  • dropped without being sent anywhere, if it holds hateful slurs or threats, or repeats a report already received within 24 hours.

This is a decision made automatically. You can ask a person to look at it again, and learn what information and which reasons led to it: write to bonjour@rayelinux.com.

What is posted, and what never is

A published report holds what happened, the steps, the language and a short name of the browser and system (for example "Firefox 160 on Linux"). Members of the Discord server can read it and reply. How to reach you is never posted: it only goes to the private staff channel. The page address and the full browser string are never posted either.

Where, how long, on what basis

My server keeps none of it, except, in memory and for 24 hours, a fingerprint of the text to spot duplicates (the text itself can't be recovered from it). Your IP address is used, in memory and for one hour at most, to limit the number of reports (3 per hour). My server is the one that sends the report to Discord: Discord does not see your IP address. Discord is a company in the United States, so the report, posted or held, is kept outside Québec, at Discord, until it is deleted.

The basis: your consent, given by sending the form, to post the report and reach you; and my legitimate interest in keeping the forum readable and free of abuse, for the check. To have a report or your contact details removed, write to bonjour@rayelinux.com.

Server logs

The server that receives visits (one I host at home, in Québec) writes down, for each request, the IP address, the date, the page asked for, the page you came from and the browser. The site's own server writes only the page, the result and the time taken, with no address and without the rest of the page address (where email link tokens travel). The site's service writes counters (questions received, reports sent), never a text, an email address or an IP address.

These logs are for security and troubleshooting (legitimate interest). The front server is copied every night, and those copies are kept for 7 days on a disk at home.

Still to fix: today the front server's logs are not deleted automatically yet; they go back to October 30, 2026. I am setting up their deletion after 14 days. Until then, you can ask me to delete the lines about your address.

Cloudflare and downloads

Every connection to the site goes through Cloudflare, Inc., a company in the United States, which encrypts it and shields the site from attacks. Cloudflare decrypts the connection on its servers, then passes it to my server through an encrypted tunnel: what you send to the site, including in the help bubble and the bug form, therefore passes through Cloudflare's servers.

The ISO file is served from Cloudflare's storage (dl.rayelinux.com). Cloudflare therefore sees your IP address and the pages and files you ask for, and this can be processed outside Québec and Canada, under its own privacy policy. Cloudflare tries to add an audience measurement script to the pages; the site's security policy blocks it, so it never runs.

The basis: my legitimate interest in serving the site safely and fast.

Cookies and browser storage

The site uses no cookies, no pixels, and no third-party fonts or scripts. It does not measure its audience.

One thing only can stay in your browser: if you pick a language with the language button, that choice is kept in local storage (key "raye-lang", value "fr" or "en"), on your device only. The rayelinux.com address reads it to open the right language for you. It is never sent to the server. To clear it, clear the site's data in your browser.

The help bubble and the download page's tick box keep nothing: everything is gone when you close the page.

The Discord server

The community meets on a Discord server. Discord is a third-party service with its own terms and privacy policy, and you must be the age it requires to join. What you write there is seen by the server's members and by Discord. Announcements are posted there by RivenBot, a Discord bot I also make; what it keeps is described in its own policy, on rivenbot.ca.

Moderators can delete messages and remove members under the server's rules. You can delete your own messages at any time.

Applications to join the team

The "Join the team" pages have a form. When you send it, I keep, on my server in Québec:

  • the role asked for and the page language;
  • your email address, to answer you (or, once Raye accounts open, your account id instead);
  • your Discord username, to give you the role;
  • what you write: languages, what you're good at, why you want to help;
  • the version of the consent text you ticked, and the dates: sent, decided, role given;
  • the decision and who on the team made it.

I do not keep your IP address: it is only used, in memory and for one hour, to limit the number of applications per visitor.

Who reads it

A message goes to a private Discord channel of the Raye Team, so it knows an application is waiting. It holds the role, your Discord name, your languages, what you're good at and your text. It does not hold your email address, which stays on my server. Discord is in the United States: see the section on transfers below. Only the people on the team who review applications can see it, and they commit to doing nothing else with it.

Your choices

Sending an application is a choice: nothing on the site or in Raye depends on it. You can withdraw your consent at any time by writing to bonjour@rayelinux.com; your application is then deleted and, if you had been given a role, it is removed. You can also ask for a copy or have it corrected, as the section on your rights says.

The Community page

The Community page shows how many members the Discord server has and how many are online. My server asks Discord for these two numbers, every ten minutes, through the public invite address; your browser does not contact Discord. Nothing about you is sent or kept for it. If you click "Join the Discord server", you go to Discord, and its own rules apply.

The beta program and update channels

The beta program goes through the applications described above. Today, being accepted gives a role and a channel on Discord. Raye Update will later have channels (stable, public beta, beta, dev); access to the non-public ones will be kept for accepted people. That mechanism is not built yet: before it opens, this section will say exactly what Raye Update sends to prove access, and nothing will be sent before then.

Whatever the channel, update checks stay the ones of the section "What Raye Linux contacts from your PC": a download, with no usage report.

When you write to me

An email sent to bonjour@rayelinux.com arrives on my mail server in Québec, which is copied every night to a disk at home. I keep it while I answer and follow up your request; you can ask me to delete it afterwards. For a request about your rights, I keep a record of the request and my answer, because the law can ask me to show that I answered.

Raye accounts (once they open)

Raye accounts are not open yet: no account server is running. What follows describes what an account will keep, from its code. Raye works the same without an account, and nothing in the system needs one.

  • Kept: your email, your name, your picture (redrawn as a 256-pixel image, which removes its GPS location and other data), a closed list of 153 Raye settings (appearance, taskbar, language and region, accessibility and a few more), your Store app library and one row per PC.
  • Never readable: your password, tokens and recovery codes are only kept as hashes; your two-step secret is sealed with a key that is not in the database.
  • Not kept: your IP address, what you browse, your files, what you run, when you use your PC. The server keeps no request log. Wi-Fi and other passwords: not synced.
  • The accounts site will set one session cookie and, if you pick a language, one cookie that remembers it; no other.
  • You will be able to export everything as one file and delete the account in one go, with your password. The basis will then be performing the service you ask for.

Before they open, this section will be completed (where the server runs, how long backups last after a deletion) and dated in the history below.

What Raye Linux contacts from your PC

  • Raye Update downloads Raye's updates from dl.rayelinux.com (Cloudflare) and raye-linux.github.io (GitHub), and the base system's updates from its official servers, since Raye is based on Ubuntu 26.04 LTS.
  • Firmware updates come from the public LVFS service, when you accept them.
  • The Store contacts Flathub for apps and odrs.gnome.org for reviews.
  • Firefox updates from Mozilla's repository.

Like any download, these servers see your PC's IP address and what is downloaded, under their own rules. Raye sends no usage reports and no crash reports: the tools that did are removed from the image. What you do on your PC stays on your PC.

Nobody but me has access to the mailing list, the logs and my servers. The only companies on the path of your information are:

These countries don't have the same laws as Québec: a foreign government can, under its laws, ask for access to information a company keeps there. I keep what goes there to what is needed: nothing from the mailing list or the help bubble is kept by these companies, and a bug report only goes there if you send it.

I will only give your information to an authority if a law requires it, and I will tell you if the law allows me to.

How your information is protected

  • Each service runs in its own machine, behind its own firewall; the site's server only accepts the front server, and every administration connection goes through a gateway that records it.
  • The mailing list database is only readable by the site's service; the mail settings are read as secrets and never written to logs.
  • Email link tokens are only kept as hashes, and expire.
  • The site forbids any script, font or image from elsewhere, and its pages can't be shown inside another site.
  • Connections are encrypted from your browser to my home.

No measure is perfect. If you find a security hole, write to me at bonjour@rayelinux.com before talking about it publicly, so I can fix it.

Your rights, and how to use them

You can, at any time and at no cost:

  • know whether I keep information about you, and see it;
  • have it corrected if it is wrong, incomplete or unclear;
  • have it deleted, and its spread stopped (a posted report, for example);
  • get a copy of it in a common, machine-readable format;
  • withdraw your consent (every email has a one-click unsubscribe link);
  • be told about a decision made automatically about you, its reasons, and ask a person to look at it again;
  • in the European Union, also object to processing based on legitimate interest and ask for it to be restricted.

How to do it

  1. Write to bonjour@rayelinux.com, preferably from the address concerned.
  2. Say what you want: see, correct, delete, get a copy. For a bug report, give its title or date.
  3. I may ask you to show that the address or the report is yours, and nothing more.
  4. I answer in writing within 30 days. If I refuse, I tell you why and what you can do about it.

For the mailing list, the unsubscribe link is enough: it asks for nothing else and works at once.

If my answer doesn't satisfy you

You can complain to the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (cai.gouv.qc.ca). Elsewhere in Canada, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. In the European Union, you can complain to your country's data protection authority.

Security incidents

If an incident affects your information (unauthorized access, loss or leak), I take steps at once to limit it and keep it from happening again. If it carries a risk of serious harm, I tell you and the Commission d'accès à l'information without delay. Every incident, even one with no serious risk, is written down in an incident register.

Children

The site is not meant for children and does not knowingly collect anything about them. If you are under 14, a parent or guardian must consent for you before you join the mailing list or send a bug report. If you think a child gave me information without that consent, write to me: I delete it.
